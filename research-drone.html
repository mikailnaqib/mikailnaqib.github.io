Lightening the Load

The PhD student behind this project is a good friend of mine I met through Comet Aerobotics. He knew I was good at CAD, so when he needed to put some electronics on his drone, he asked me to help, and I said yes. It was my first time working in this lab.

His research needed a drone that could carry a set of antenna equipment: a Raspberry Pi, an Ettus USRP B206mini-i, an antenna array, and a power splitter, about 750 g all together. The lab had a completely stock 500 V2 quadcopter, but its frame was too heavy to carry all of that.

So I took the whole drone apart, weighed every part, and looked for what I could replace and where. I swapped the thick carbon fiber legs for much thinner ones and cut down the frame plates. Together, those changes gave the drone 12% more payload capacity.

The electronics needed somewhere to live though, so I designed a mounting platform in SolidWorks that sits under the frame on the drone's two existing tube mounts, with cages that hold each piece of equipment. Since the drone only flies in very specific conditions, the cages just need to hold everything in place, not protect it. Everything was 3D printed in PLA.

The only real iteration was getting the electronics cages to fit. Once the fitment was right, connecting everything was pretty easy, and the first version took less than a month.

The drone has flown with the new setup, and the lab is using it for research. The project also introduced me to Mr. Nayan, another member of the lab, and I'm now working with him on my current lab project, copper antenna FDM fabrication.

The drone project isn't finished, either. The lab is getting new electronics, and I'm still helping design the housings for them.
